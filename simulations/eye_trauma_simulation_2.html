How a blow near the eye can tear the retina

The inside of the eye is filled with a clear jelly, the vitreous gel. It is attached to the retina, the thin light-sensitive layer lining the back of the eye. When something hits the bone next to the eye, the eye is jolted. The gel is heavy and lags behind, so it can pull away from the retina. If it stays stuck at one spot, it can rip a small tear in the retina. This is more likely in older eyes, because the gel becomes more watery and loosens with age.

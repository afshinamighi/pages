Grover Search Lab

Imagine searching an unsorted list of 32 items for the one that meets a condition, where the only tool is a check that says yes or no for a single item. A classical computer has to test items one by one: about 16 checks on average, and all 32 in the worst case, when the hidden item happens to be the last one tested. Grover's algorithm needs 4 iterations, always. There is no lucky or unlucky case, because every iteration acts on all 32 items at once, so the count is the same wherever the hidden item sits. That fixed count grows only with the square root of the list size. It starts by putting 5 qubits into an equal superposition of all 32 items. It then repeats two steps: the oracle marks the hidden item by flipping the sign of its amplitude, and diffusion reflects all amplitudes around their average. Each round moves a little more amplitude onto the marked item, so a measurement after the right number of rounds almost certainly returns it. On this page you apply these operations yourself, one at a time or one iteration at a time, step back and forward through the circuit, and watch how the 32 amplitudes change. The gate-level circuits at the bottom show what the oracle and diffusion are made of.

How to use this page
  1. Choose a target (optional). Flip the bits or click a bar. The default is |10110⟩.
  2. Press H on all once to start.
  3. Press Oracle, then Diffusion. That is one iteration. Full iteration does both in one click.
  4. Repeat step 3 until you reach 4 iterations.
  5. Press Measure to read the result.
  • The highlighted button marked ← next is always the next step.
  • ◀ Back and Forward ▶ (or the ← → keys) step through the gates you applied.
  • Run to optimum plays all the steps for you. Reset starts over.

Inside the oracle

Oracle for |10110⟩

  • X gates on the qubits where the target has a 0, so the target becomes |11111⟩.
  • Multi-controlled Z flips the sign only when every qubit is 1.
  • X gates again undo the first step. Only the target's sign has changed.

Inside diffusion

Diffusion (same for any target)

  • H then X on all qubits turns the equal superposition into |11111⟩.
  • Multi-controlled Z flips the sign of that one state.
  • X then H undo the change of view. The net effect flips every bar around the mean.
